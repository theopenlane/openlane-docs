# Quickstart Guide

Welcome to Openlane. The fastest way to understand the platform is to build something real: stand up your compliance program in four phases, each one building on the last.

<Steps>

1. [**Configure your organization**](#1-configure-your-organization): access, authentication, and your team
2. [**Represent your organization in the Registry**](#2-represent-your-organization-in-the-registry): the systems, vendors, and people your program describes
3. [**Establish your compliance posture**](#3-establish-your-compliance-posture): your policies and the standards you're working toward
4. [**Align your program**](#4-align-your-program): controls grounded in your policies, and a plan for proving they operate

</Steps>

## Configure Your Organization

Start with how your team accesses Openlane. The full configuration guide lives in [Settings](./settings/overview.mdx); the short version:

- **Set up authentication.** Add [allowed email domains and auto-join](./settings/authentication/sso.mdx#getting-people-into-your-organization) if you're not using SSO, or configure your identity provider through [Single Sign-On](./settings/authentication/sso.mdx) if you are. Only turn on **Enforce SSO** after a successful test connection; the organization owner is always exempt, so you can't lock yourself out.
- **[Invite your team](./settings/organizations/members.mdx#inviting-members)** across Engineering, Security / IT, HR / People Ops, and Leadership, and use [groups](./settings/groups/overview.mdx) to organize who owns what. There are no per-user fees, so bring in everyone the program touches.

## Represent Your Organization in the Registry

Before writing policies or controls, give your program something concrete to describe. The [Registry](./registry/overview.mdx) is your source of truth for who is involved, what you rely on, and what is in scope. Everything you build later points back at these records.

**Navigate to:** `Registry`

- Add your [**Platforms**](./registry/platforms.mdx): the systems and boundaries that define your compliance scope, along with their system details
- Add your [**Vendors**](./registry/entities.mdx): the third parties your business depends on (your cloud provider, payroll, monitoring, and so on)
- Add your [**Personnel**](./registry/personnel/overview.mdx): employees and contractors who work in the environment
- Add your key [**Assets**](./registry/assets.mdx): the systems, services, and devices you need to govern

## Establish Your Compliance Posture

With the foundation in place, define what your organization intends to do and what you're working toward.

### Write your policies

Policies define intent; they're the commitments your controls will operationalize.

**Navigate to:** `Policies & Procedures`

- [Import existing policies](./compliance-management/policy-and-procedure-management/import.mdx) if you have them (Markdown, Word, or synced from GitHub)
- Or generate new ones using Openlane's integrated AI, then edit them to match reality

### Add a standard

Next, define what you're working toward.

**Navigate to:** `Standards Catalog`

- Click `Details` and select a relevant domain. For SOC 2, these are the Trust Services Criteria (remember, Security is the only required TSC)
- Select and enable a framework: SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, ISO 42001, and more
- You can enable **multiple frameworks** without duplicating work; one control can satisfy requirements across several

## Align Your Program

Now connect the pieces: controls that put your policies into practice, mapped to your standard, with a plan for proving they operate.

### Create your controls

Controls are the backbone of your program. They define how you actually meet requirements, grounded in the policies you just wrote.

[Full guide](./compliance-management/controls/import.mdx)

- Clone controls from your enabled standard's catalog, or [import controls](./compliance-management/controls/import.mdx) you already have
- Link each control to the policies it operationalizes and map it to your framework(s)
- Assign owners: the people from your Registry personnel records who actually run each process

### Establish evidence collection

Evidence is how you prove controls operate over time. Decide upfront how you'll collect it, rather than scrambling at audit time.

- Review the [evidence guide](./compliance-management/evidence/overview.mdx) and attach [evidence](./compliance-management/evidence/create.mdx) to your controls as you go
- Use [integrations](./integrations/overview.mdx) to pull posture data automatically where you can
- Use [tasks and automation](./automation/overview.mdx) to put recurring collection (access reviews, policy attestations) on a cadence

## What's Next

Now that you have the foundation:

- Refine controls to match how your team actually works
- Create a [program](./compliance-management/programs/overview.mdx) to track your first audit period
- Explore [**Exposure**](./exposure/overview.mdx) to manage vulnerabilities, findings, and remediations
- Stand up your [**Trust Center**](./trust-center/overview.mdx) to share your posture with customers
