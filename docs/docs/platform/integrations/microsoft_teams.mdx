# ![microsoft teams integration](./assets/microsoft_teams.png) Microsoft Teams integration guide

If your team works in Microsoft Teams, this integration posts Openlane notifications to a Teams channel, so they reach the people responsible for them where they already work.

## Key capabilities

Openlane posts notification messages to a team and channel you choose, and you set a default channel for any message that does not name one. You can send a test message at any time to confirm the connection works.

Openlane posts as the Microsoft account that connected the integration and can only reach the teams and channels that account can post to. Connect with an account that belongs to every team you want messages in, such as a shared compliance account, rather than one person's account.

## Prerequisites

* A Microsoft work or school account that is a member of the teams you want Openlane to post in
* If your organization does not let users consent to apps on their own, an administrator who can grant consent to the Openlane app

## Step-by-step setup

### Step 1: Connect in Openlane

<Steps>

1. Go to **Automation** > **Integrations**, find **Microsoft Teams**, and click **Configure**.
1. Click **Continue to Authorization**. You will be redirected to Microsoft.
1. Sign in and approve the requested permissions. Openlane asks to read your profile and the teams and channels you belong to, and to send channel messages.
1. You are redirected back to Openlane and the connection is saved.

</Steps>

You do not need to create an app registration or enter a tenant ID. Openlane reads your tenant from the account you sign in with.

### Step 2: Choose a default channel

<Steps>

1. On the **Installed** tab, open the Microsoft Teams card and click **Set Default Channel**.
1. Choose a channel from the list of teams and channels your account can post to, then save.
1. Optionally click **Send Test Message** to post a message to the channel and confirm it arrives.

</Steps>

If you connect more than one Microsoft Teams account, turn on **Default Messaging** for the one Openlane should use when a message does not name a connection.

## Validate connection

After saving, Openlane runs a health check that confirms it can sign in to Microsoft as the connected account and displays the result on the **Installed** tab of the Integrations page. You should see a **Healthy** badge. If the badge shows **Needs Attention**, review the troubleshooting section below.

## What Openlane syncs

Openlane does not import data from Teams. It reads the list of teams and channels the connected account can post to, so you can choose where messages go, and sends outbound messages to those channels. Use it to route compliance notifications to the people who act on them, which helps you show an auditor how <Term t="finding">findings</Term> reach the responsible team.

## Disconnect

To remove this integration, go to **Automation** > **Integrations** and select the **Installed** tab. Open the menu on the integration card and select **Disconnect**. Openlane removes its stored credential and stops sending messages. To revoke access fully, remove the Openlane app from **Enterprise applications** in Microsoft Entra ID.

## Troubleshooting

If Microsoft blocks the sign-in with a consent message, your organization requires an administrator to approve new apps. Ask a Microsoft Entra ID administrator to grant consent to the Openlane app, then connect again.

A channel that does not appear in the list belongs to a team the connected account is not a member of, or one where it cannot post. Messages also stop when the connected account is disabled or removed from a team, so disconnect and connect again with an account that has access.
