# Vendor scoring

Vendor scoring is the assessment step of <Term t="third-party-risk-management">third-party risk management (TPRM)</Term>. You answer the same risk questions for each [vendor](./entities.mdx), and Openlane turns the answers into a risk score and rating. Where a vendor's tier says how much scrutiny it warrants, the rating records what that scrutiny found.

## How a score is calculated

Each answer scores **impact × likelihood**, and the vendor's risk score is the total across its answers. Impact is how much damage the gap would cause with this vendor, and likelihood is how probable that is.

<VendorRiskMatrix />

On a Yes / No question, **Yes** means the control is in place and scores 0. **No** scores impact × likelihood, and so does any answer to a text, number, or pick-one question.

The total then maps to a rating. A vendor gets the first rating whose upper score it does not exceed:

| Rating | Default score |
| --- | --- |
| <RatingChip rating="None" /> | 0 |
| <RatingChip rating="Very Low" /> | Up to 3 |
| <RatingChip rating="Low" /> | Up to 5 |
| <RatingChip rating="Medium" /> | Up to 11 |
| <RatingChip rating="High" /> | Up to 15 |
| <RatingChip rating="Critical" /> | Above 15 |

For example, a vendor answers No to two questions and Yes to the rest. Enforcing multi-factor authentication is rated Critical impact and Medium likelihood (5 × 2 = 10), and testing the disaster response plan is rated High impact and Low likelihood (4 × 1 = 4). The vendor scores 14, which is High.

## Scoring a vendor

Scoring works without any setup, using 37 built-in questions. Answering them takes edit access to the vendor, and changing how scoring works takes an organization role:

| To do this | You need |
| --- | --- |
| Answer questions, upload answers, or set a score by hand | Edit access to the vendor |
| Read a vendor's answers and notes | View access to the vendor |
| Change questions, the unanswered setting, or rating bands | The organization owner or admin role |

<Steps>

1. Open the vendor from **Registry > Vendors** and select the **Risk Review** tab.
1. Under **Risk Scoring**, click **Start scoring**. On a vendor that already has answers, the button reads **Edit answers**.
1. Answer the questions in each category. For anything other than Yes, choose a **Likelihood**, and change **Impact** if this vendor's exposure differs from the default.
1. Add **Notes** with the reason for the rating, such as the section of the vendor's SOC 2 report you relied on. A reviewer or auditor reading the score later sees the reasoning next to it.
1. Click **Done**.

</Steps>

Complete answers save as you go, and the score at the top of the panel updates with them. An answer that still needs a likelihood is not saved, and you are warned before you close. To remove an answer, select the pressed **Yes** or **No** again, or click **Clear answer** on other question types.

Back on the **Risk Review** tab you should see the answer score, how many questions are answered, and which categories the score comes from. Expand a category to read its answers and notes.

<VendorScoringPreview view="results" />

### Have the vendor fill in the answers

When the vendor is the one who knows the answers, exchange them as a CSV file. Both options are in the menu next to **Start scoring** or **Edit answers**.

<Steps>

1. Choose **Download questions (CSV)** and send the file to your [vendor contact](./contacts.mdx).
1. When the file comes back, review the `Answer` column and set `Likelihood` on every answer that is not Yes. Likelihood and impact are your judgment of the risk to your organization, so we recommend setting them yourself instead of asking the vendor to.
1. Choose **Upload completed questionnaire** and import the file.

</Steps>

Rows with no answer are skipped, so a partly completed file still imports. If any answered row has a problem, such as a missing likelihood, nothing is imported and the error names the rows to fix. Importing a file again updates existing answers without duplicating them.

### Set a vendor's score by hand

Enter a score yourself when you assessed a vendor outside these questions.

<Steps>

1. Open the vendor and select the **Risk Review** tab.
1. Under **Risk summary**, edit **Risk Score** and press Enter.

</Steps>

The next change to the vendor's answers replaces a score entered by hand. To keep hand-entered scores for every vendor, choose **Set ratings by hand** in the [scoring settings](#unanswered-questions).

### Send the questions from Openlane <StatusChip status="coming-soon" />

You will be able to send the scoring questions to a vendor and have them answer in Openlane. Today, responses to a [questionnaire](../automation/assessments.mdx) sent through a campaign are kept with the campaign and do not change the vendor's score.

## Configuring scoring for your organization

The questions, the unanswered setting, and the rating bands apply to every vendor, so scores stay comparable.

<Steps>

1. Go to **Registry > Vendors**.
1. In the banner above the table, click **Customize scoring**. Once scoring is set up, the button reads **Configure scoring**.
1. Make your changes on the **Questions** and **Scoring & rating bands** tabs.
1. Click **Save changes**.

</Steps>

If the banner is hidden, open the **Risk scoring** gear menu on the Vendors page and choose **Configure risk scoring**.

### Questions

Turn off questions that do not apply to your vendors, edit the built-in ones, or add your own. You can change a built-in question's wording and default impact, but not its category or answer type. **Reset to default** in the question editor restores the Openlane version.

<VendorScoringPreview view="questions" />

Write Yes / No questions so that Yes is the safe answer, because Yes always scores 0. "Is customer data encrypted at rest?" scores correctly. "Has the vendor had a breach in the last year?" would score a breach as no risk.

To add a question:

<Steps>

1. On the **Questions** tab, click **Add question**.
1. Enter the **Question**, and use **Why it matters** to explain it to whoever answers.
1. Choose a **Category** and a **Default impact**. The default impact is the starting point on each vendor, and you can change it when answering.
1. Choose an **Answer type**. **Pick one** needs at least two answer options.
1. Click **Add question**, then **Save changes**.

</Steps>

Question changes apply to answers recorded from then on, and existing answers keep the wording they were given under. Once a question you added has been answered for a vendor, its answer type is fixed and it cannot be deleted. Turn it off instead.

### Unanswered questions

| Setting | Effect on the score | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ignore unanswered questions** (default) | Only answered questions count | Answers arrive over time |
| **Count unanswered as highest risk** | Each unanswered question adds 20 | Every vendor must answer everything |
| **Set ratings by hand** | Answers are kept but not totaled | You score vendors outside these questions |

With **Ignore unanswered questions**, read the score alongside its coverage. A vendor with two answers and a score of 0 has not been shown to be low risk.

Before you choose **Count unanswered as highest risk**, raise the rating bands. One unanswered question adds 20 points, which is Critical under the default bands.

With **Set ratings by hand**, Openlane does not total answers or apply the rating bands. A vendor's score and rating stay as set when its answers or the bands change.

### Rating bands

Each band sets the highest score that earns its rating, and each must be higher than the one before it. A score of 0 is always None, and anything above the High band is Critical.

<VendorScoringPreview view="bands" />

Scores are totals, not averages, so the default bands are strict: two No answers at High impact and Medium likelihood already add up to 16, which is Critical. We recommend scoring a few vendors you know well first, then adjusting the bands until their ratings match your judgment.

### What changes a score

| Change | Effect on scores |
| --- | --- |
| Saving, changing, or clearing an answer, its likelihood, or its impact | Recalculates that vendor |
| Saving new rating bands or a new unanswered setting | Recalculates every scored vendor |
| Adding, editing, or turning off a question | Leaves existing scores alone. With **Count unanswered as highest risk**, a vendor picks up the change the next time one of its answers changes |
| Entering a score by hand | Replaces that vendor's score until the next recalculation |
| Changing the vendor's tier | None |

With **Set ratings by hand**, none of these recalculate a vendor, and only a score you enter changes it.

## Finding vendors by rating

Once scoring is set up, **Vendors by risk rating** above the Vendors table shows how many vendors hold each rating and how many are not scored. Select a rating to filter the table to those vendors. The table also has **Risk Rating** and **Risk Score** columns, hidden until you turn them on.

A high rating is a finding to act on. Record the decision in a vendor review on the **Risk Review** tab, and add anything that needs treatment to your [risk register](../exposure/risks/risk-register.mdx).

## Standard questionnaires and references

A vendor may already have completed an industry questionnaire. Ask for it before sending your own questions, use its answers to score the matching ones.

| Source | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| [Consensus Assessment Initiative Questionnaire (CAIQ)](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/artifacts/star-level-1-security-questionnaire-caiq-v4-1) | Yes / No questions on cloud provider security controls, from the Cloud Security Alliance (CSA) |
| [CSA STAR Registry](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/star/registry) | Looking up whether a vendor has already published a CAIQ |
| [Standardized Information Gathering (SIG) questionnaire](https://sharedassessments.org/about-sig/) | A broad third-party questionnaire from Shared Assessments, available by subscription or membership |
| [Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Toolkit (HECVAT)](https://www.educause.edu/higher-education-community-vendor-assessment-toolkit) | Assessing vendors that serve colleges and universities, from EDUCAUSE |
| [Minimum Viable Secure Product (MVSP)](https://mvsp.dev/) | A short security baseline for lower-tier vendors |
| [NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) | Guidance on conducting risk assessments |

For what to review beyond the questions, see [Reviewing a vendor](./entities.mdx#reviewing-a-vendor).

## FAQ

<details>
<summary>Why did a risk score I entered by hand change?</summary>

Openlane recalculates the score and rating whenever a vendor's answers change, or when someone saves new rating bands or a new setting for unanswered questions. A score entered by hand stays in place if the vendor has no answers or if your organization uses **Set ratings by hand**.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why does the answer score differ from the vendor's risk score?</summary>

The answer score on the **Risk Review** tab adds up the answers alone. The vendor's risk score can also include points for unanswered questions, and it can be a value someone entered by hand.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can the same question carry a different impact for different vendors?</summary>

Yes. The impact on a question is a starting point, and you can change it on each vendor. Missing encryption matters more for a vendor that stores customer data than for one that does not.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How does vendor scoring differ from risk scoring?</summary>

A vendor's risk score is calculated from its answers. A [risk](../exposure/risks/overview.mdx#scoring-risks) in your register has a score you set on the risk itself, and it uses its own impact and likelihood levels. The two do not update each other, so a high vendor rating does not create or change a risk.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can I see how a vendor's answers changed over time?</summary>

Yes. When an answer has changed, the question shows the earlier answer and its date in the answer panel.

</details>
