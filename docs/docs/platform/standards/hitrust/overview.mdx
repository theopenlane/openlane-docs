# HITRUST

The HITRUST Common Security Framework (CSF) harmonizes more than 60 authoritative sources, including <Term t="health-insurance-portability-and-accountability-act">HIPAA</Term>, National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) publications, ISO 27001, Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), and <Term t="general-data-protection-regulation">GDPR</Term>, into a single control library, so one assessment can demonstrate coverage against many underlying requirements. Certification is issued by HITRUST itself after a validated assessment, which gives the resulting report more consistent rigor than self-attested questionnaires.

## What the HITRUST CSF Is

The CSF is a control library, not a law. HITRUST curates requirements from the underlying frameworks, updates them against current threat intelligence (HITRUST describes the framework as threat-adaptive), and packages them into assessments of increasing depth. Assessments are scoped and executed through MyCSF, HITRUST's assessment platform, and validated assessments are performed by assessor firms that HITRUST authorizes. Because the library maps back to its sources, a single certified assessment can support reporting against multiple regimes at once, which is the framework's main efficiency argument.

## Assessment Tiers

HITRUST offers three assessment levels. The tiers are nested, so work completed for a lower tier carries forward when you move up.

| Tier | Requirements | Certification Validity | Designed For |
| ---- | ------------ | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| **e1 (Essentials)** | 44 curated requirements (CSF v11) covering essential cybersecurity hygiene | 1 year | Startups, low-risk vendors, and organizations new to HITRUST |
| **i1 (Implemented)** | 182 requirements reflecting leading security practices | 1 year, with a streamlined rapid recertification option in year two | Organizations with an established security program that need credible third-party validation |
| **r2 (Risk-based)** | Tailored by risk and compliance factors, typically several hundred requirements | 2 years, with an interim assessment at the one-year mark | Organizations with significant regulatory obligations or high inherent risk; the most rigorous HITRUST assurance |

The tiers also differ in how they are scored. e1 and i1 evaluate whether controls are implemented. r2 uses HITRUST's control maturity model, scoring policy, process, and implementation for each requirement, which is a materially heavier lift in both documentation and evidence.

## Who Pursues It

HITRUST certification is a market requirement rather than a legal one. It is most common among organizations that handle protected health information: health systems, payers, and especially the vendors that sell to them, since large healthcare enterprises frequently require HITRUST certification in contracts as a condition of doing business. Adoption has been spreading to other industries that want a certifiable, framework-spanning assessment. Note that HITRUST certification does not itself constitute HIPAA compliance; the CSF maps to HIPAA requirements, but HIPAA remains a separate legal obligation.

## Certification Process

The process runs through MyCSF and an authorized External Assessor:

1. **Scope and license.** Define the assessed environment in MyCSF and generate the requirement set for your chosen tier.
2. **Readiness.** Optionally run a self-assessment against the same requirements to find gaps before paying for validation.
3. **Validated assessment.** An authorized External Assessor firm tests and scores the requirements and submits the assessment to HITRUST.
4. **HITRUST quality assurance.** HITRUST reviews the submission for consistency and scoring accuracy; expect follow-up questions.
5. **Certification.** HITRUST issues the report and certification, with corrective <Term t="action-plan">action plans</Term> for any gaps that scored below threshold. Maintain it through annual recertification (e1, i1) or the interim assessment (r2).

Timelines vary with scope and readiness; e1 efforts commonly run a few months, while first-time r2 certifications frequently take a year or more.
