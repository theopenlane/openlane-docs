# NIST 800-53

NIST 800-53 is a cybersecurity standard and compliance framework developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). It outlines a set of security controls for federal information systems, designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data by mitigating risks from various threats through specific controls like access management, awareness training, and incident response procedures.

## Risk-based approach

The framework encourages organizations to assess their risks and implement controls based on their specific needs and environment.
It is primarily used by US government agencies to ensure compliance with security regulations.

## Continuously updated

NIST regularly updates the standard to reflect evolving cybersecurity threats and technologies.

## Revision 5

NIST Special Publication 800-53 Revision 5, titled "Security and Privacy Controls for Information Systems and Organizations," is the current version of the catalog. The changes from Revision 4 are structural. The word "federal" was dropped from the title because the catalog is now written for any organization that adopts it, not just federal agencies. Control statements became outcome-based: "the organization" was removed as the subject of each control, so a control now states what must be achieved rather than which entity does it. Privacy controls, formerly a separate appendix, were integrated into the main catalog, and two new families were added, Supply Chain Risk Management (SR) and PII Processing and Transparency (PT). The control baselines moved out of the catalog into a companion publication, covered below.

For more details, see the [official NIST SP 800-53 Revision 5 publication](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final).

## Companion Publications

Two companion documents shape how the catalog is applied in practice. NIST SP 800-53B defines the low, moderate, and high impact control baselines (plus a privacy baseline) that determine which controls apply to a given system, and NIST SP 800-53A defines the assessment objectives and procedures assessors use to verify that each control is implemented as intended. If you are preparing for a FISMA or FedRAMP assessment, the 800-53A assessment objectives are what your evidence will be judged against.

## System Security Plans

The Planning (PL) family expects each in-scope system to have a system security plan (SSP): a formal document describing the system and the controls that protect it. An effective SSP:

- Defines the system boundary, identifying which components are inside it
- Records the system's security categorization for confidentiality, integrity, and availability, following FIPS 199 and NIST SP 800-60
- Describes the system's operational context and its relationships to other systems
- Summarizes the security requirements and lists the controls in place or planned to meet them
- Is approved by an authorizing official, reviewed at least annually, and kept consistent with your enterprise architecture

We recommend treating the SSP as a living document: distribute it to the personnel who need it, protect it from unauthorized access, and update it as the system and its controls change. The controls it describes should reflect a defense-in-depth approach, with multiple coordinated layers rather than reliance on any single safeguard.
